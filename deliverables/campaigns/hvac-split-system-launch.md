**Client:** licensed electrician, residential split-system installation
**Prepared:** September 2026 · **Owner:** marketing VA · **Review gate:** end of week 2
**Built with:** `.claude/skills/hvac-growth-marketing`

## Read this first — the offer as briefed cannot fund paid acquisition

The brief sets a target CAC of **$150–200 per completed install** against a gross profit
of **$567**. Those two numbers are incompatible with Brisbane search costs.

Working the funnel at realistic rates:

| Stage | Rate | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | — | $4,000 |
| CPC — "aircon installation brisbane" and siblings | ~$12 (est. range $8–20) | 333 clicks |
| Click → qualification started | 25% | 83 |
| Qualification → photos uploaded | 60% | 50 |
| Photos → price presented (**qualified lead**) | 90% | 45 |
| Price → deposit started | 30% | 13.5 |
| Deposit started → deposit paid | 80% | 11 |
| Scheduled → **completed install** | 95% | **10.3** |

- Cost per qualified lead: **$89**
- **CAC per completed install: $389** — roughly 2× the briefed target
- Contribution after CAC at $567 GP: **$178/install**
- Marketing efficiency: **1.46×**

A 1.46× business is technically profitable and practically fragile. One callback, one
site that turns out non-standard, one week of peak-season CPC inflation, and a job's
entire contribution is gone. The $150–200 CAC target would require ~20% click-to-lead
*and* ~40% lead-to-deposit simultaneously. Possible after optimisation. Not a plan.

**The fix is not cheaper clicks. It is a higher gross profit per job.**

The $1,999 single-head unit is the *hook*, not the sale. Two changes make the same $389
CAC comfortable:

1. **Attach priced add-ons at the point of price presentation** — extra pipe run,
   second-storey access, core drilling, old-unit removal, condensate pump, and above all
   **the dedicated circuit / switchboard work**.
2. **Quote multi-head at the same visit.** A homeowner air-conditioning a bedroom in
   September is air-conditioning the living room by December. Ask on the form.

At a blended ASP of ~$2,900 and blended GP of ~$1,050:

- Contribution after CAC: **$661/install** (3.7× better)
- Marketing efficiency: **2.7×**
- Break-even CAC rises from $567 to $1,050 — real headroom to compete

**This is the highest-leverage decision in the whole campaign, and it is an offer-design
decision, not a media decision.** Everything below assumes it is made.

### The electrician angle is the moat — lead with it

Most aircon installers sub out or coordinate the electrical work. That is the single most
common source of quote revisions and surprise costs, and switchboard/circuit work is the
highest-margin add-on in the job. One licensed trade owning both the install and the
wiring is a true, checkable, customer-visible claim that no competitor running the same
price offer can make.

> **Installed and wired by the same licensed electrician. No second trade. No surprise
> switchboard cost.**

### Timing — launch now, scale in November

Brisbane residential demand ramps from October, peaks November–January, and CPCs inflate
materially into the peak. September is the correct launch month: buy the learning cheaply,
bank reviews and real installation photography, then scale a proven funnel into the peak.
A funnel launched in December pays peak prices for education.

Establish the true, measured cost per **completed paid installation** on Google Search

Not: leads, clicks, impressions, or a full calendar of unprofitable jobs.

## OFFER

**3.5 kW reverse-cycle split system — $1,999 supplied & installed.**
Fixed price. No quote appointment. Book online.

Add-ons priced and disclosed on the price page (never hidden, never a surprise):

| Add-on | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Pipe run beyond 3 m, per metre | Most common non-standard variable |
| Second-storey / height access | Ladder vs. scaffold changes labour |
| Double-brick or concrete core drilling | Older Brisbane housing stock |
| New dedicated circuit + RCBO | **The electrician's margin** |
| Switchboard upgrade | **The electrician's margin** |
| Old unit removal + degassing | Requires ARCtick handling |
| Condensate pump | Where gravity drain isn't available |

### "Standard installation" — must be published, in plain English

Before a single dollar is spent, this definition goes live on the landing page as a
linked, readable page. Under the ACCC single-price rule, "$1,999 installed" must be
genuinely achievable for a standard install with the exclusions clearly disclosed. If
most jobs can't be done at the advertised price, the ad is misleading no matter what the
fine print says.

A standard installation is:

- Back-to-back, or up to 3 m of pipe run
- Single-storey, ground floor, indoor head on an external wall
- Outdoor unit on a ground pad or low bracket, reachable from a ladder
- Existing spare capacity at the switchboard, or an existing dedicated circuit
- Brick veneer, weatherboard or Hebel wall — not double brick, not solid concrete
- Safe ladder access, no scaffolding required
- No asbestos present
- No removal or disposal of an existing unit

Asbestos wall → decline and refer. Do not price it online.

### Compliance to verify before launch — blocking

- [ ] **ARCtick licence** held (or a named licensed refrigerant handler engaged) for
      refrigerant work and old-unit degassing
- [ ] **QLD electrical licence** number available for display
- [ ] Warranty wording matches the actual manufacturer + workmanship terms
- [ ] Review and install counts are genuine and evidenced
- [ ] Public liability insurance current

No ad copy makes a licensing, warranty or accreditation claim that isn't on this list.

## TARGET CUSTOMER

**Primary — Brisbane homeowner with immediate or near-term need.** Buying triggers:

- Hot bedroom / another bad night's sleep
- New baby or children's bedroom
- Home office that's unusable in the afternoon
- Existing system has failed or is struggling
- Renovation, new room, or just moved in
- Deliberately preparing for summer

They are not shopping for "quality HVAC services". They have a hot room and no appetite
for three days of quote theatre.

**Secondary — investors and property managers.** Different logic entirely (depreciation,
tenant retention, minimum housing standards, multi-property rollout). Own landing page,
own campaign — **after** the primary engine converts. Not in scope for weeks 1–4.

## GEOGRAPHY

**Do not run all of Brisbane. Do not run SEQ.**

Launch on **one dense cluster, ~10 km radius**, chosen so a crew completes 2–3 jobs per
day without crossing the city.

**Recommended: inner-north / north-west Brisbane.**
Chermside · Aspley · Stafford · Everton Park · Kedron · Nundah · Wavell Heights ·
Geebung · Zillmere · Bracken Ridge · Gordon Park · Stafford Heights

Rationale: dense post-war and pre-2000 housing stock, high proportion of homes without
ducted, strong retrofit rate, owner-occupier majority, and tight enough to route
efficiently.

Track **cost to serve by cluster**, not just CAC by cluster. A suburb with equal CAC and
40 minutes of extra drive time is a worse suburb.

Expand only when demand exceeds crew capacity inside the cluster — not because the
account "could reach more people".

## CHANNEL

**Phase 1 (weeks 1–4): 100% Google Search.** Not the briefed 60/25/15.

At $1,000/week, a three-way split buys three unreadable channels. Search is the only
channel where a homeowner with a hot bedroom declares intent, and it's the only place
where four weeks of this budget produces a statistically usable cost-per-install. Get
that number first; it is the denominator for every later decision.

**Phase 2 (weeks 5–8), gated on Phase 1 clearing threshold:** introduce Meta prospecting
(25%) and retargeting (15%) — by which point there are real reviews, real installation
photography, and a retargeting pool worth the name. Meta prospecting with no proof and no
audience data is paying to educate strangers.

### The qualification flow — the actual product

This is where the campaign is won or lost. Six questions, then two photos.

1. Which room? *(bedroom / living / office / other)* — routes capacity
2. Roughly how big is the room? *(3 size bands with visual reference)*
3. Single or double storey, and which floor is the room on?
4. Wall type? *(brick veneer / weatherboard / double brick / concrete / not sure)*
5. Is there an existing unit to remove?
6. Any other rooms you'd want done at the same time? **← the ASP lever**

Then: **photo of the inside wall** + **photo of the outside wall and the switchboard**.

Then: **the price**, on screen, itemised — base $1,999 plus any add-ons the answers
triggered. Then a deposit button and a date picker.

## MEASUREMENT

**No spend goes live until the full funnel is instrumented.** A price-led campaign with
partial tracking optimises toward whichever stage happens to be measured.

Events to GA4 + Google Ads, with values:

| Event | Value | Primary conversion? |
|---|---|---|
| `qualification_started` | — | No |
| `photos_uploaded` | — | No |
| `price_presented` | AUD price shown | No |
| `deposit_started` | — | No |
| `deposit_paid` | AUD deposit | **Yes — optimise to this** |
| `install_completed` | AUD invoice total | Imported offline |
| `review_left` | — | No |

Offline conversion import is what closes the loop between a deposit and a *completed,
invoiced, profitable* job — including the add-ons. Without it, Google optimises toward
deposits on jobs that turn out non-standard and get refunded.

Also required: call tracking on the header number, and a mandatory "how did you hear
about us" field at deposit for cross-check against attribution.

## SUCCESS THRESHOLD

Reviewed at end of week 4. All four must clear to proceed to Phase 2:

1. **≥8 completed installations** from $4,000 spend *(CAC ≤$500)*
2. **Blended gross profit ≥$900/install** — the attach rate is working
3. **Cost per price-presented ≤$110**
4. **Callback / rework rate ≤10%** of completed jobs

Clearing all four → Phase 2: raise Search spend, add Meta prospecting and retargeting.

**Failing #2 → stop and fix the offer, not the ads.** Single-unit economics do not
survive scale, and more spend against a $567 gross profit buys a bigger fragile business.

**Failing #1 or #3 while #2 clears** → the funnel is the problem, not the economics.
Diagnose the specific stage: click→qualification is a landing-page and message-match
problem; photos→price is a flow-friction problem; price→deposit is a pricing or trust
problem. Fix one stage, re-measure.

**Interim gate at end of week 2:** actual CPC vs. the $12 estimate. If CPC ≥$18, pause,
tighten geography and keywords, and re-plan before spending the back half.
