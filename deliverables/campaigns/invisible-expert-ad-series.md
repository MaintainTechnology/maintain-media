# THE INVISIBLE EXPERT
### Maintain Media — lead generation ad series
**Goal:** 5 new retainer clients. **Owner:** Marketing VA (production + publishing), Lead-gen VA (DMs + booking). **Jon:** approvals and calls only.

---

## 1. The strategy in one page

### Who we are actually talking to
Not "SMBs." The **owner-operator who IS the brand** — founder, principal, director, or senior expert in an established business (roughly $1m–$10m revenue, or a senior ERP/enterprise consultant). They have 15–25 years of real expertise, a referral-fed pipeline, and a LinkedIn profile they last touched years ago.

They are not "looking for a social media manager." They would never search that. They are quietly frustrated that people with a fraction of their credibility are getting the recognition, the inbound, and the work.

### The enemy (this is the emotional engine of the whole series)
> **Being the best-kept secret in your industry is not humility. It's a tax — paid every month in work that went somewhere else.**

We are not fighting other agencies. We are fighting **the injustice of the invisible expert**: the best operator in the market losing to the loudest one. That is the feeling every ad in this series presses on.

### What we sell (never say "social media management")
| Say this | Never say this |
|---|---|
| We build your reputation | We manage your social media |
| Your name becomes the one they call first | We grow your followers |
| Your voice, written for you | Content creation |
| A calendar with buyers in it | Engagement and reach |

### The four things that make us different — every ad carries at least one
1. **We build the person, not the logo.** Nobody has ever bought from a logo. Competitors post for a company page; we build an individual's authority.
2. **The Blind Test.** We calibrate a voice engine on how you actually think and speak, then prove it: three drafts, two ours, one yours. If you can pick ours, we're not finished. Everyone else hands over AI slop.
3. **We finish the job — attention becomes meetings.** The appointment-setting add-on means content ends in a booked call, not a like. Almost no content agency will touch this.
4. **We ran the experiment on ourselves first.** 172,000 organic impressions in 12 months, zero ad spend, 9–16% engagement, +29.6% follower growth in 30 days. Our case study is our own founder, not a stranger's screenshot.

### Recommended channel — one approach, not five
Run the series as **LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads from Jon Pepper's personal profile**, not the Maintain Media company page.

Why: the ads for a personal-branding service should themselves *be* a personal brand working. Every impression is a live demonstration of the product. Thought Leader Ads also carry materially higher engagement and lower CPC than company-page ads, and they mean the prospect lands on a human being — which is the whole thesis.

Support it with the same copy published organically on Jon's feed (free, and it feeds the ad account's best performers).

**Planning budget:** AU$50/day for 30 days ≈ **AU$1,500**, split 60% cold (Acts I–II), 40% retargeting (Acts III–IV). Retargeting is where the cheap conversions live — never underfund it.

## 3. Publishing sequence — 30 days

| Week | Cold (Acts I–II) | Retarget (Acts III–IV) | VA action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ads 1, 2, 3 | — | Build engager list, warm the audience |
| 2 | Ads 4, 5, 6 | Ad 8 | DM everyone who engaged in week 1 |
| 3 | Ad 7, best of wk 1–2 | Ads 8, 9, 10 | DM + book; push Founding Five |
| 4 | Winners only, scaled | Ads 10, 11, 12 | Close-out on the five spots |

Post organically the day before boosting — organic performance tells you which ad to put money behind. Never boost an ad that flopped for free.

**Kill rules.** After 1,000 impressions: under 2% engagement, kill it. After AU$100 spend with no profile visit or lead, kill it. Reallocate to the winner — do not "give it more time."

## 4. Appointment-setting playbook — the lead-gen VA runs this

The ads create signal. This converts it. **Meetings are booked by a human, not a form.** That is the differentiator; it is also the operating instruction.

### Daily list (15 minutes)
Every morning pull, from Jon's LinkedIn notifications and ad reporting:
1. Anyone who **commented** on an ad or organic post.
2. Anyone who **reacted twice or more** in the last 14 days.
3. Anyone who **viewed the profile** after an ad ran.
4. Anyone who **followed** in the last 7 days.

Qualify before you write: owner / founder / director / principal, business looks $1m+ or a senior consultant, and they are visibly the face of what they sell. If not, skip. Volume is not the goal here — five clients is.

### Message 1 — same day as engagement (never a pitch)
> Hi [First name] — saw you'd read the piece on [topic]. Curious, because you're in [industry]: is [the specific problem the post named] actually how it plays out for you, or is that just how it looks from the outside?

**Rule: the first message must contain a genuine question you would want an answer to.** No "I noticed you engaged with our content." That reads as automation and burns the lead permanently.

### Message 2 — after they reply
> That tracks. Most of the people we work with say the same thing.
>
> Quick context on why I asked — we build the LinkedIn presence for founders and specialists who are excellent at the work and invisible online. Jon does the same thing on his own profile: 172k impressions last year, no ad spend.
>
> We're doing a free written feature for five people at the moment, published to our ERP audience — no charge, no obligation, you just get a published article out of it.
>
> Worth 30 minutes with Jon to see if you're one of the five?

### Message 3 — the booking ask (always offer two specific times)
> Jon has Tuesday 10am or Thursday 2pm free. Which suits?
>
> Here's his calendar if easier: [link]

### If no reply
Day 4: *"No stress if the timing's off — worth me checking back in a month?"*
Day 14: send a relevant post or the Humans of ERP feature, no ask.
Then stop. Two follow-ups, not seven. We are building a reputation, not a spam operation.

### Three qualifying questions before the call is confirmed
1. Are you the one who decides marketing spend?
2. Roughly how much of your work comes from referral today?
3. Have you tried posting yourself, or had someone do it for you before?

Answers go in the CRM before the call. Jon walks in already knowing the answer to all three.

### Handover to Jon
The night before, the VA sends: name, business, role, what they engaged with, their answers to the three questions, and one sentence on what they seem to actually want. Nothing else.

## 5. What "it worked" looks like

Working backwards from **5 retainers**, at honest conversion rates:

| Stage | Target | Assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified DM conversations | 120 | ~30/week |
| Meetings booked | 24 | 20% of conversations |
| Meetings held | 17 | 70% show rate |
| Retainers signed | **5** | 30% close, helped by the free feature |

**Weekly scoreboard (VA reports every Friday):** conversations opened, replies, meetings booked, meetings held, signed. Five numbers. Nothing else on the report.

**Revenue at target:** 5 × AU$1,000/month = **AU$5,000 MRR**, plus 5 × AU$400 setup = AU$2,000 once. Appointment-setting add-ons and website/newsletter add-ons lift the average retainer materially — expect the real figure to land higher than $1k if the Act IV ads do their job.

**Planning assumptions, flagged:** the 20% / 70% / 30% rates are industry-typical for warm, personally-messaged founder audiences with a genuine free-value offer. They are estimates, not measured. Replace them with real numbers after week 2 — if meetings booked runs under 20%, the problem is the DM opener, not the ads.

## 6. Production notes — keep it on brand

Read `DESIGN.md` and `design-system/index.html` before making a single graphic.

- Deep teal-black `#08282d` ground, purple `#a04dff` for one accent per graphic, white text. Never purple on purple.
- Albert Sans. Large. One idea per graphic — if it needs two sentences to read, it is two graphics.
- **Banned:** stock handshakes, people pointing at whiteboards, gradient mesh blobs, "AI" iconography, emoji-led copy, tiny tracked uppercase eyebrow labels above every heading.
- **Preferred:** real portraits, real screenshots, real numbers set large, honest empty space.
- The ads for a personal-brand service must look like they came from a person. Where an ad can carry Jon's actual face instead of a graphic, use the face.
